# Fanfare — User Guide

Everything the app does, from the player's side. Fanfare is standard chess
with one twist: before the game, you and your opponent each secretly arrange
your own bishops and knights, and both setups are revealed at the same
moment.

## Starting a game

1. On the **Games** screen, tap **New game** (or **Start your first game**).
2. You land on the invite screen with your game code (something like
   `happy-tiger`). Tap **Share link** to send it with your phone's share
   sheet, or **Copy code** to paste it anywhere.
3. The screen updates by itself the moment your friend joins — no refresh
   needed. Colours (White/Black) are assigned randomly at that moment.
4. Changed your mind? **Cancel game** in the header ends it (with a
   confirmation).

## Joining a game

- **From a link**: opening the link lands you on the Join screen with the
  code already filled in — tap **Join game**.
- **From a code**: tap **Join with code** on the Games screen and type it.
- You can't join your own game, and a game someone else already joined
  tells you so in plain words.

## The setup phase

- Your four back-rank squares between the fixed pieces hold two knights and
  two bishops, starting in the normal chess arrangement. Tap two highlighted
  squares to swap what's on them. Any arrangement is fine — even both
  bishops on the same colour.
- Your opponent **cannot see your choice** — their four squares show
  face-down tokens on your screen too.
- Tap **Confirm setup** when you're happy. It locks — no changes after.
- When both players have confirmed, both setups appear at once and the game
  begins. White moves first.
- During setup, either player can still cancel the game from the header.

## Playing

- **Your turn** shows above the board. Tap one of your pieces to see its
  legal moves as dots (rings on captures); tap a dot to move. Tap the piece
  again to deselect.
- Moves appear on your opponent's board live. If you're Black, the board is
  flipped so your pieces are at the bottom.
- A pawn reaching the last rank asks which piece it becomes.
- The last move is highlighted; a king in check glows red.
- **The bottom panel** appears once both setups are locked in. It shows the
  last move; tap or drag it up for the full move list and captured pieces;
  drag it all the way down to leave the game (your game is always waiting
  in the Games list).

## Rematch

On a finished game's screen, **Rematch** starts a new game against the same
opponent with the colours swapped — no new code to share, it goes straight to
the setup phase. If your opponent already started one, the button reads
**Open rematch** and takes you to it (you both always land in the same
game, even if you tap at the same moment). A game that was cancelled before
anyone joined has no opponent, so it has no rematch button.

## Draws, resigning, endings

- **Draw** (header) offers a draw; your opponent gets Accept / Decline
  buttons. Making any move withdraws a pending offer. You can't accept your
  own offer.
- **Resign** (header) ends the game — your opponent wins. It asks first.
- The game ends by itself on checkmate, stalemate, threefold repetition,
  the 50-move rule, or insufficient material — the result banner says what
  happened in plain words.

## Chatting during a game

Once someone has joined, a **speech-bubble button** appears — bottom right on
a phone, next to the game's other buttons on a wide screen. Tap it and the
chat slides in from the side. A dot on the button means your opponent said
something you haven't read; it clears when you open the chat. The chat stays
available after the game ends, so you can say "good game".

Two ways to say how you mean it, both chosen **before** you send — a message
can't be edited once it's away:

- **Tags** — up to two words describing what you mean: happy, sad, serious,
  joking, sarcastic, sincere, or any word you type yourself. They appear
  above your message and colour its edge.
- **Stress** — up to two words you'd hit if you were saying the sentence out
  loud. "I *never* said that" reads differently from "I never said *that*".
  Marked words show in italics with a hand-drawn stroke under them.

Tap the tag button to the left of the box to open both pickers: your words
appear as chips you can tap to unmark, and the message sends with them.

**Reactions** are the after-the-fact half: the small **+** under any message
opens six emoji, and tapping one you already used takes it back. Your
opponent sees it straight away.

Chat belongs to one game. Starting a rematch starts a fresh conversation.

## Practice on your own

**Practice** (in the bottom bar, or the button under your games) is a game
against the board itself — no friend, no code, no connection needed. It works even
when the app can't reach the internet.

- Pick how strongly the board plays — the pieces from pawn (**Beginner**)
  up to queen (**Deep** — it takes a moment longer between moves; the
  chosen level's name shows under the row) — then your colour (tap the
  white or the black pawn) and arrange your bishops and knights as in a
  real game. Two buttons under the board begin it: **Start** plays what
  you chose, and **Random** skips the choosing — it draws your colour and
  your layout for you and starts on the spot. Either way the secret
  arrangements reveal with the same flourish.
- While the board is thinking, the status line says so — on a slow phone a
  long think shows a seconds counter, so it never looks stuck.
- **Take back** (under the board) undoes your last move and the board's
  reply — practice is for trying things. The result line at the end says
  how many you used, in brackets ("Checkmate — you won! (2 takebacks)"); a
  game played straight through says nothing. **Start over** (header)
  discards the game and returns to the setup choices.
- A practice game survives closing or reloading the app — you're asked
  nothing, it just continues, even mid-think. It never appears in your
  games list, and finishing one offers **Practice again**.

## The Games list

Each game shows its code and a badge: **Waiting for a friend**, **Set up
your pieces**, **Waiting for their setup**, **Your turn**, **Their turn**,
or the result (**You won** / **You lost** / **Draw** / **Cancelled**).

## Your identity

There are no accounts. Your games belong to this device (a hidden anonymous
identity created on first launch). Clearing site data or uninstalling loses
access to them — there is no recovery, by design.

## Turn alerts

Menu → **Turn alerts** asks your browser's permission once, then notifies
you when it's your move — including when a friend joins your game, the
setups reveal, a rematch is waiting, or a game ends. Off until you turn it
on. Tapping a notification opens the right game. If the row says
**Blocked**, your browser is refusing notifications for this site — allow
them in the browser's site settings, then toggle again. (On iPhone/iPad,
notifications need the app added to your Home Screen first.)

## The menu

**How to play** (the one-screen explainer), **Game sounds** (soft
move/capture/check taps — off until you turn them on), **Turn alerts**
(above), dark/light mode, automatic-update settings, and
**Install app** for adding Fanfare to your home screen.
